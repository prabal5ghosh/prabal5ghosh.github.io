Research

FSHD is a muscular dystrophy that progresses slowly and affects individual muscles at different rates, which makes change between consecutive visits difficult to assess. My doctoral research addresses this by modelling several types of data collected longitudinally on the same patients.

Data

The first source is whole-body muscle MRI, acquired with Dixon and STIR sequences, from which quantitative biomarkers are derived: muscle fat fraction, muscle fat infiltration and lean muscle volume. The second is the set of clinical and functional measures recorded at each visit. The two sources are complementary and evolve on different timescales.

Method

Each patient is represented by a latent disease state that evolves according to coupled latent ordinary differential equations. Each modality has its own dynamics, and explicit coupling terms relate changes observed on MRI to changes in clinical scores. Interpretability is a central requirement: the parameters of the model should be clinically meaningful and open to inspection.

Missing data

Longitudinal clinical cohorts are rarely complete: visits are missed and not every measure is acquired at every visit. I am investigating mask-aware representation learning and generative imputation with variational autoencoders, so that the model distinguishes observed values from missing ones.
